SECTION 1 — PREAMBLE AND INTRODUCTION

1.1  About the Institution — Drona Education Limited (Skill College)
Skill College is the brand name of Drona Education Limited, a company incorporated under the Companies Act and engaged in the promotion and delivery of Skill-Based Higher Education across India. Drona Education Limited was established in 2015 and has since grown into a leading platform for skill-based vocational higher education, currently operating across multiple states in India with 10+ Skill Hub Centres, 2000+ industry tie-up partners (Skill Knowledge Providers), 200+ team members and faculty, and over 3400 active vocational trainees. The Institution is actively expanding its presence to additional states and institutions pan-India.
The Institution's academic programmes are offered through a network of affiliated Hub Centres across India, each affiliated with a recognised and UGC-approved university. Each hub centre delivers industry-integrated degree programmes that carry full university recognition and UGC approval. The current affiliated universities include Silver Oak University (SOU), RK University (RKU), Noble University (NU), Sigma University (SU), Bhagwan Mahavir University (BMU), and Gujarat Technological University (GTU). As the Institution expands, additional universities and hub centres shall be added to the Schedule of Hub Centres (Section 3), and the provisions of this Policy Booklet shall apply to them equally.
The word 'Skill' as used in connection with programme names (e.g., 'Skill BBA', 'Skill BCA') is exclusively a BRAND DIFFERENTIATOR used by Drona Education Limited to distinguish these vocational, practice-first programmes from traditional classroom-only programmes. The official UGC-recognised degree nomenclature for all programmes remains unchanged as per UGC guidelines — i.e., BBA, BCA, B.Com, MBA — and these degrees carry full academic recognition for higher studies, government examinations, and employment, identical to any university-issued degree of the same name.
The Institution's distinguishing feature is the mandatory On-the-Job Training (OJT) component, wherein students are placed with registered employer organisations known as Skill Knowledge Providers (SKPs). SKPs play a dual role: as training hosts who provide students with real-world professional experience, and as evaluators who award performance-based marks (SKP Marks) that form a component of the student's academic assessment.

1.4  What is a Skill Degree? — The Academic Philosophy
A Skill Degree is a UGC-approved, NEP 2020-aligned degree programme built on the globally proven German Dual VET (Vocational Education and Training) System. This system has been instrumental in virtually eliminating unemployability in Germany and Austria, and is the model adopted by the world's highest-performing economies including the United States, United Kingdom, and Germany, where the highest proportion of students opt for skill-based or vocational degree programmes.
Unlike traditional degree programmes that are theory-heavy and industry-disconnected, a Skill Degree is structured on a 40:60 curriculum model — 40% theoretical classroom education and 60% hands-on practical training at industry. This dual structure transforms the student into both a qualified graduate and a working professional simultaneously:

A formal written notice issued to a student by the Placement Team or the Institution for non-compliance with placement obligations or for misconduct. Accumulation of three warning letters results in blacklisting.

The action of temporarily or permanently suspending a student's access to placement services through the Application, as a consequence of serious non-compliance or repeated violations of placement policy.

CGPA

Bonafide Certificate
An official document issued by the Institution confirming that the named individual is a bona fide student enrolled in a specified programme.

First Year / Second Year / Third Year of the enrolled programme respectively.

The following conditions further govern late entry:

4.3  Attendance for Online Lectures
Where online or recorded lectures are delivered, attendance eligibility is subject to the student's active participation for more than 80% of the lecture duration. Students who join late, leave early, or do not actively participate for the required proportion shall not receive attendance credit for that session.
Additionally, students must ensure their full name and specialisation are correctly entered in the attendance system. Incorrect or incomplete information may render the student ineligible for attendance in that session.
4.4  Attendance Grievance and Correction Procedure
Students who believe their attendance has been incorrectly recorded must adhere to the following strict procedure:
(a)  Any attendance dispute must be raised through the official ticketing system (Application) within TWENTY-FOUR (24) HOURS of the relevant lecture or session. Disputes raised after this deadline shall not ordinarily be considered for attendance correction under any circumstances.
(b)  The student must provide complete details of the lecture, including the date, day, subject, and faculty name, when raising the dispute.
(c)  The Institution shall verify the dispute with the concerned faculty. If the faculty confirms the student's presence, attendance shall be updated within 72 hours. If the faculty is unable to confirm the student's presence, attendance shall not be credited, and the decision of the faculty shall be final.
(d)  Attendance shall not be granted for any session in which the student was not physically or virtually present, regardless of the reason for absence.
IMPORTANT:  Attendance disputes raised after 24 hours of the lecture date shall be closed without review. Students are advised to check their attendance status after every class.

4.5  Medical Leave and Health-Related Absence
A student who is absent from the institution on account of illness or a medical condition may apply for medical leave, subject to the following conditions:
(a)  Medical leave is recognised only after a minimum consecutive absence of FIFTEEN (15) days, except at PCM, where medical documents may be submitted to the admin office within 7 working days from the date of return for attendance consideration.
(b)  The student must submit original medical reports, prescriptions, and/or hospital records to the Centre Head. Photocopies shall not be accepted unless certified by the issuing doctor or hospital.
(c)  A formal written application for medical leave, bearing the student's signature and endorsed by the Head of Department (HOD), must accompany all medical documents.
(d)  When the student resumes attendance, medical reports signed by parents must also be submitted to the college staff room and emailed to the Centre Head for verification.
(e)  The Centre Head shall evaluate the genuineness of the application. If the application is found to be genuine, the Centre Head shall forward it to the university for consideration of attendance condonation.
(f)  Submission of medical documents shall not automatically guarantee restoration of attendance or exemption from minimum attendance requirements. The final decision rests with the affiliated university.
(g)  Communication with the Centre Head throughout the leave period is mandatory. Students who fail to maintain timely communication during their absence shall have their leave applications rejected.
4.6  Company Tour and Off-Campus Absence
Absences on account of company tours, field visits, or off-campus programmes shall be treated as follows:
(a)  The company or institution conducting the tour must provide an official letter on its letterhead, bearing the authorised signature and stamp, along with an accompanying email confirmation addressed to the Centre Head.
(b)  Company tour absences are sanctioned for a maximum of EIGHT (8) to TEN (10) consecutive days per engagement. At SBS-affiliated hubs, a maximum of TWO (2) days off per week may be sanctioned for company tours. Extensions beyond these limits require prior written approval from the Centre Head.
(c)  Applications for company tour leave must be submitted BEFORE the commencement of the tour, not retrospectively.
4.7  Semester-Long Absence
A student who is absent from the institution for an entire semester — for any reason whatsoever — shall be subject to the following consequences:
(a)  The student shall remain liable to pay the full tuition fees prescribed for that semester. Non-payment shall attract penalties as per the fee policy.
(b)  The student shall be INELIGIBLE to appear in the regular (main) examinations for that semester.
(c)  The student may be eligible to appear in supplementary examinations for that semester, subject to meeting the eligibility criteria as determined by the affiliated university and the Institution's eligibility assessment.
(d)  No exemption from academic obligations or fee payment shall be granted on the grounds of involvement in any external activity, employment, personal commitment, or Skill Knowledge Provider (SKP) obligations, unless the absence has been formally pre-approved in writing by the Centre Head and the Dean of the affiliated university.
(e)  A student seeking to regularise a semester-long absence must submit a formal explanation and supporting documentation to the admin office. Failure to do so shall result in the student being ineligible for examinations and may lead to cancellation of enrolment.


SECTION 5 — FEES, PAYMENTS, AND FINANCIAL OBLIGATIONS

5.1  Student's Obligation to Pay Fees
Payment of all prescribed fees — including tuition fees, examination fees, NSDC fees, and any other dues — is a mandatory and unconditional obligation of every enrolled student. The obligation to pay fees is not contingent upon the student's attendance, academic performance, placement status, or satisfaction with any service provided by the Institution.
Fees once paid are non-refundable except in accordance with the refund provisions of the affiliated university's regulations and the Institution's Admission Cancellation Policy (refer Section 13).
5.2  Fee Circulars and Communication
Fee circulars are issued separately for FY, SY, and TY students at the commencement of each semester. The Fee Circular is the authoritative document for fee amounts, payment schedules, and deadlines for the respective semester and year. Circulars are communicated through the following channels:
Official WhatsApp groups maintained for each class or batch.
Google Classroom posts by the Centre Head or designated faculty.
College website (applicable at PCM).
Students are responsible for monitoring these communication channels regularly. Claims of non-receipt of fee circulars through the above official channels shall not constitute grounds for exemption from fee payment obligations or waiver of late fee penalties.
5.3  Late Fee Application and Penalty
A student who is unable to pay fees by the prescribed deadline must comply strictly with the following procedure:
(a)  The student must submit a formal written application for late payment to the Centre Head or the Admin Team BEFORE the last date of fee payment. Applications submitted after the fee deadline shall not be considered.
(b)  The application must clearly state the reason for the delayed payment and include any relevant supporting documents.
(c)  At hubs affiliated with SOU, BMU, and SBS, the Centre Head shall forward the application to the respective university or institution for approval before any late payment arrangement is confirmed.
(d)  A late fee penalty shall be imposed on ALL students whose fees are paid after the prescribed deadline, regardless of whether a late payment application has been submitted and regardless of the reason for delay. The penalty amount is as specified in the relevant university or institution circular.
(e)  Submission of a late payment application and/or payment of the late fee penalty does not guarantee relief from any academic consequences that may arise from fee non-payment (e.g., exclusion from examination).
(f)  At PCM, late fee applications may be submitted online with written justification and relevant proof of hardship or emergency.
5.4  Post-Dated Cheque (PDC) Policy
Where a student is permitted to submit a Post-Dated Cheque (PDC) as a deferred payment instrument, the following conditions shall apply:
(a)  PDCs must be submitted to the Centre Head and the Admin Department by the deadline specified in the relevant circular. CHEQUES WILL NOT BE ACCEPTED AFTER THE STATED DEADLINE.
(b)  Each PDC must clearly state the reason or purpose for which it is being submitted (e.g., Semester 1 tuition fee, examination fee, etc.).
(c)  Submission of a PDC must be accompanied by a formal late fees application as described in Article 5.3.
(d)  In the event that a PDC is dishonoured upon presentation, the student shall be liable for the full outstanding amount plus any banking charges incurred, and the matter shall be treated as a default in fee payment, with all associated consequences.
5.5  GrayQuest EMI Financing Facility
The GrayQuest EMI facility is available to eligible students as an option to pay tuition fees in equated monthly instalments. The following terms and conditions govern the use of this facility:
(a)  Students must apply for GrayQuest through the official Skill.college website within the timeline specified in the relevant circular. Applications outside this window shall not be processed.
(b)  Approval of GrayQuest applications is subject to the credit assessment and policies of GrayQuest as an independent third-party financing provider. The Institution does not guarantee approval of any application.
(c)  The following documents are mandatory for GrayQuest application: (i) Positive CIBIL score report of parent or guardian; (ii) Aadhaar Card; (iii) PAN Card; (iv) Bank Statement of parent or guardian.
(d)  EMI dates and payment schedules shall be communicated through the GrayQuest portal. Students are responsible for monitoring their GrayQuest portal for updates on dues and upcoming instalments.
(e)  Non-payment of any GrayQuest instalment by the due date shall be treated as default in fee payment and may result in the recovery of the full outstanding amount.
(f)  At Sigma University, GrayQuest is processed in two instalments. Students must confirm the current split arrangement with the Centre Head.

SECTION 9 — ON-THE-JOB TRAINING (OJT) AND PLACEMENT POLICY

9.1  Mandatory Nature of OJT
Participation in the On-the-Job Training (OJT) programme is a MANDATORY academic requirement for all enrolled students. OJT is not an optional add-on but a core component of the vocational curriculum. Failure to actively engage with the OJT and placement process shall have direct academic consequences, including failure in Vocational Practice and ineligibility for VIVA.
Every student is expected to maintain active placement status through the Skill College Application throughout their academic tenure. The student's obligation to pursue placement commences from the First Year and continues until the completion of the programme.
9.2  The Role of the Skill Knowledge Provider (SKP)
The Skill Knowledge Provider (SKP) is the company or employer organisation at which the student undertakes OJT. The SKP plays the following roles in the student's academic programme:
(a)  TRAINING HOST: The SKP provides the student with a real-world professional work environment, practical training, and exposure to industry practices relevant to their specialisation.
(b)  EVALUATOR / MARKS AWARDER: The SKP awards SKP Marks to the student based on the student's attendance, performance, attitude, and conduct during the OJT period. These SKP Marks are a graded component of the student's university examination assessment (Vocational Practice) and are entered into the university's ERP portal by the hub administration.
(c)  DOCUMENT ISSUER: The SKP is responsible for issuing official documents including the Consent Letter (LOC), providing company marks on the Attendance Sheet, and certifying the student's Workbook.
Students must maintain a professional, diligent, and respectful relationship with the SKP at all times. The SKP's assessment of the student has a direct bearing on the student's academic result.
9.3  Letter of Intent (LOI)
The Letter of Intent (LOI) is a formal document that serves as the first step in registering an unregistered company on the Application system. The following terms govern the LOI process:
(a)  The LOI is required ONLY when a student undertakes self-placement at a company that is NOT already registered in the Application.
(b)  The Placement Relationship Officer (RO) or the student (with Placement Team assistance) obtains the LOI from the company and uploads it to the Application to create the company profile.
(c)  The LOI is submitted ONCE per company engagement. If the company is already registered in the Application, no LOI is required.
(d)  The LOI format is provided by the Placement Department. Use of any other format shall not be accepted.
9.4  Letter of Consent (LOC / Consent Letter)
The Letter of Consent (LOC) is a mandatory document confirming the Skill Knowledge Provider's (SKP's) acknowledgement and consent for the student's placement at their organisation. The following rules govern LOC submission:
(a)  The LOC must be submitted at the commencement of each academic year or at the time of joining any new company, whichever occurs first.
(b)  If a student switches to a new company during an academic year, a fresh LOC must be submitted for the new company immediately upon joining.
(c)  If the academic year changes shortly after a company switch, the student must submit a SECOND LOC for the new academic year, even if they remain with the same company.
(d)  The LOC must be obtained on the company's official letterhead, bearing the authorised signature and stamp of the company representative.
(e)  Students must upload the LOC to the Application AND submit a physical hard copy to the designated Placement Representative at the hub centre.
(f)  The LOC may be substituted by an official Offer Letter from the company in situations where the company declines to issue a Consent Letter.
(g)  For outstation internships or placements, the LOC must additionally be accompanied by parental or guardian consent.
9.5  OJT Attendance Sheet Requirements
Students on OJT must maintain and submit a monthly attendance sheet. The following requirements are MANDATORY and non-negotiable:
(a)  FORMAT: The attendance sheet must be in the prescribed format provided by the Placement Department. Sheets in any other format shall be rejected.
(b)  SIGNATURES: The attendance sheet must bear (i) the signature of the authorised Supervisor at the SKP/company; (ii) the official stamp of the company; and (iii) the student's own signature.
(c)  CONTENT: The sheet must clearly state the total gross working hours and dates for the respective period.
(d)  SUBMISSION DEADLINE: Attendance sheets must be submitted within the FIRST WEEK of the subsequent month at the latest. Sheets submitted after this deadline SHALL NOT BE ACCEPTED under any circumstances, regardless of the reason for delay.
9.6  Workbook Requirements
The Workbook is a fortnightly log of the student's OJT activities and learnings. The following rules apply:
(a)  A Workbook entry must be prepared for every FIFTEEN (15) days of internship. Gaps in Workbook maintenance shall reflect adversely on the student's VIVA assessment.
(b)  The Workbook must be uploaded to the Application with the student's digital signature.
(c)  At the conclusion of the OJT engagement with the company, the complete Workbook must be stamped and signed by the company's authorised representative.
(d)  The Workbook is one of the mandatory documents for VIVA eligibility. Absence of a completed, signed Workbook shall render the student ineligible for VIVA.
9.7  Project-Based OJT
A project-based alternative to standard OJT (SKP placement) may be assigned under the following circumstances:
(a)  FIRST YEAR STUDENTS: First Year students who are unable to secure a placement at a Skill Knowledge Provider within the designated placement period shall be assigned a project by the Placement Department via official email and WhatsApp communication.
(b)  SECOND YEAR AND ABOVE — COUNSELLED CASES: Students from SY onward who, after appropriate counselling, are assigned a project-based OJT shall NOT be eligible to receive a final institutional placement through the Placement Team for the relevant semester.
(c)  FINAL YEAR INTERNSHIP SUBJECT: All Final Year (TY) students — whether currently placed at a company or not — are required to undertake a mandatory project as part of the Internship subject. This is a separate academic requirement and is not a substitute for active OJT placement.
9.8  Changing the Skill Knowledge Provider (Company)
A student who intends to change their current Skill Knowledge Provider must follow this procedure without exception:
(a)  The student must first raise the matter with the Placement Mentor or the Placement Team, providing a clear, genuine, and documented reason for the requested change.
(b)  The Placement Team shall evaluate the reason and attempt to resolve any issues the student may have with the current company before approving a change.
(c)  If the reason is found to be genuine, or if the Placement Team is unable to resolve the student's concern, the student shall be guided to formally resign from the current company through the Application and the process for finding a new placement shall begin.
(d)  A student must NOT resign or leave a company without prior intimation to and approval from the Placement Team. Unauthorised departure shall attract the consequences described in Article 9.9.
(e)  At SOU, a minimum internship tenure of SIX (6) months at the first company is expected. Resignation before 6 months may result in a ban. At other hubs, twelve (12) months is the standard minimum tenure.
9.9  Consequences of Leaving a Company Without Authorisation
Any student who resigns from or abandons their SKP (company) placement WITHOUT prior intimation to and approval of the Placement Team shall be subject to the following consequences:
(a)  AUTOMATIC BAN: The student's account on the Application shall be automatically flagged or banned upon submission of an unsanctioned resignation. This ban restricts the student from accessing placement-related features of the Application.
(b)  SEMESTER BAN: At RKU and NU affiliated hubs, the student shall be banned from the Placement Module for a minimum period of one (1) semester.
(c)  DELAYED REINSTATEMENT: Unauthorised departure shall delay and may result in the permanent rejection of the student's reinstatement (unban) request. The student shall be required to apply for reinstatement through the Application, and reinstatement is at the discretion of the Placement Team.
(d)  SELF-PLACEMENT OBLIGATION: In the event that the reinstatement request is rejected, the student shall be required to undertake self-placement to secure OJT, without assistance from the Placement Team.
9.10  Self-Placement Process
Students who have secured employment at a company through their own efforts (self-placement) must follow the following process to formalise the placement:
Obtain a Letter of Intent (LOI) from the company on its official letterhead.
Upload the Self-Placement application in the Skill College Application.
Await approval from the Placement Team. Self-placement is subject to review and approval.
Upon approval, upload the Letter of Consent (LOC) in the Application.
Submit a physical copy of the LOC to the designated Placement Representative at the hub.
IMPORTANT:  Self-placement applications are reviewed by the Placement Team. Not all self-placement applications may be approved. Students must not commence work at a company until their self-placement has been formally approved.

9.11  Warning Letters — Placement
Warning letters are formal notices of non-compliance with placement obligations. They are issued in the following circumstances:
(a)  INACTIVITY: The student has not applied to any company through the Application for an entire semester and remains unplaced (non-active students).
(b)  NO-SHOW FOR INTERVIEWS: The student applied to a company but failed to appear for the scheduled interview without providing a valid and documented reason.
(c)  MISCONDUCT AT COMPANY: The student has misbehaved with company staff, committed misconduct, or violated the company's conduct policies.
(d)  UNAUTHORISED RESIGNATION: The student resigned from a company without informing or obtaining the approval of the Placement Team.
Warning letters are maintained on the student's placement record. The accumulation of THREE (3) warning letters shall result in the student being blacklisted from the Placement Module, as described in Article 9.12.
9.12  Blacklisting from Placement
Blacklisting is the most severe placement-related consequence available to the Institution. A student shall be blacklisted under any of the following circumstances:
Accumulation of three (3) or more formal warning letters.
Resignation from a placement before completing the minimum required OJT tenure (12 months standard, 6 months minimum at SOU).
Rejection of a confirmed and formal job offer from a company.
Persistent misconduct or unprofessional behaviour within the company or workplace.
A blacklisted student must apply for reinstatement through the unban request process in the Application. The Placement Team shall review the unban request and, if the stated reason is found to be genuine, shall approve reinstatement. There is no guaranteed timeline for reinstatement, and repeated violations may result in permanent blacklisting.
9.13  Non-Eligibility for VIVA
A student who has not prepared and submitted the required OJT project documentation or placement documents to the Placement Team within the stipulated period shall be declared INELIGIBLE to appear in the VIVA examination for that semester. Such a student shall consequently be deemed to have FAILED the Vocational Practice component for that semester, and the result shall be reflected in the university record accordingly.
Students in this situation are strongly encouraged to approach the Placement Team or the Centre Head at the earliest opportunity to explore available remedial options.

SECTION 12 — TRANSFER POLICY

12.1  Transfer from Another Institution or University to a Hub
A student seeking to transfer from another university or institution to an affiliated Skill College hub must fulfill all of the following requirements:
(a)  ACADEMIC CLEARANCE: The student must have successfully cleared their current semester at the previous institution. No transfer shall be processed for a student with academic backlogs unless specifically approved by the Centre Head and the affiliated university.
(b)  REQUIRED DOCUMENTS: The following documents must be submitted in full at the time of transfer application: (i) Class 10th Mark Sheet; (ii) Class 12th Mark Sheet; (iii) Leaving Certificate from the previous institution; (iv) No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the previous institution; (v) Aadhaar Card (photocopy); (vi) Mark Sheets for all completed semesters (all subjects must be passed).
(c)  SEAT CONFIRMATION: A non-refundable token admission amount must be paid at the time of the transfer application to confirm the student's seat at the new hub. This amount shall be adjusted against tuition fees upon confirmation of admission.
(d)  APPROVAL: Transfer requests are subject to seat availability at the hub and the approval of the Centre Head and the affiliated university. A transfer application does not guarantee admission.
12.2  Transfer from a Hub to Another Institution or University
Students seeking to transfer from a Skill College hub to another institution or university must initiate the process through the Centre Head. The following shall apply:
(a)  The student must meet with the Centre Head and provide written reasons for seeking transfer.
(b)  An NOC from the Institution shall be required as part of the transfer documentation. The NOC shall be issued upon completion of all outstanding dues and administrative formalities.
(c)  The transfer process is governed by the affiliated university's regulations. The Institution shall provide all reasonable assistance to facilitate a smooth transfer.


SECTION 13 — ADMISSION CANCELLATION POLICY

13.1  Right to Seek Cancellation
A student has the right to seek cancellation of their admission at any point during their enrolment. However, the Institution strongly encourages students who are considering cancellation to seek academic and personal counselling before initiating the process, as cancellation has significant academic and financial implications.
13.2  Cancellation Procedure
The following procedure must be followed without deviation:
The student must visit the hub centre in person and meet with the Centre Head to discuss the reasons for seeking cancellation. The Centre Head shall offer counselling and attempt to address the student's concerns before proceeding.
If the student wishes to proceed after counselling, a formal meeting shall be arranged involving the student, their parent(s) or guardian(s), the Dean (where applicable), and the Centre Head. This step is mandatory at Sigma University (SU) and BMU.
If the decision to cancel is confirmed, the student must complete and sign the official Cancellation Form.
At Sigma University and BMU, the completed form must be accompanied by: (a) a copy of the student's bank passbook (first page); and (b) relevant medical reports, if the cancellation is sought on health grounds.
At Noble University, the cancellation form must be submitted to the University's City Campus to the designated officer.
At SOU, the student must follow the prescribed SOP for Admission Cancellation as provided by the institution.
The Centre Head or designated administrative officer shall monitor the approval status of the cancellation application on a daily basis and shall follow up with the relevant authority to ensure timely processing.
Upon cancellation approval, an NOC shall be issued to the student. Any applicable refund of fees shall be processed in accordance with the affiliated university's refund policy, ordinarily within ONE (1) MONTH of the formal approval of cancellation.
13.3  Financial Consequences of Cancellation
Students must be aware of the following financial consequences before initiating the cancellation process:
(a)  The refund of fees upon cancellation is governed by the affiliated university's refund policy, which may provide for partial or full refund depending on the timing of cancellation relative to the commencement of the academic year.
(b)  No refund shall be issued unless the formal Cancellation Form has been submitted and approved through the prescribed process.
(c)  Any outstanding dues to the Institution must be cleared before the NOC and any refund entitlement are processed.
